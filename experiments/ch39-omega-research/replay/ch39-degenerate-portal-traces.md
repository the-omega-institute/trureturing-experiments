# What separated scalar sources identify when masses coincide

Consider $N\ge2$ complex Gaussian scalars with canonical kinetic terms in four Euclidean dimensions. The distinct positive mass values and the ranks of their spectral projections $P_a$ are known. The portal $F=F^*$ multiplies one real scalar source with known normalization. For zero-background connected responses at separated points, the full ambiguity is as follows. Decompose the tuple $(P_1,\ldots,P_s,F)$ into irreducible reducing summands. Each summand may be replaced by its complex conjugate, followed by one unitary change of basis that identifies the prescribed mass projections. Responses of orders three through $4N^2-1$ suffice for this classification. This upper bound counts insertions and need not be sharp.

## Recovering the traces at low order

For any $n\ge3$, the [separation by dilation of actual source positions](ch39-portal-cycle-order.md) applies to the distinct **mass values** on the spacetime cycle edges. Summing the internal labels inside each mass space gives the accessible coefficient
$$
\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(P_{a_1} F P_{a_2} F\cdots P_{a_n}F).
$$
The proof uses the distinct decay rates for different assignments of mass values, regardless of the dimensions of their eigenspaces. The two cycle orientations contribute conjugate coefficients. Summing the displayed coefficients over every mass label gives $\operatorname{tr}F^n$.

Suppose that two portals give the same data. Their powers of orders $3,\ldots,2N+2$ have equal traces. The union of the two sets of nonzero eigenvalues has at most $2N$ distinct real elements. At those elements $\lambda$, the multiplicity differences multiplied by $\lambda^3$ have vanishing moments of degrees zero through $2N-1$. The Vandermonde matrix is invertible, so the two nonzero spectra agree with multiplicities. The known dimension also determines the zero multiplicity.

If the common nonzero spectrum is empty, both portals vanish. Otherwise interpolate the real values $1/\lambda^2$ on its at most $N$ distinct points by a polynomial $q$ of degree at most $N-1$. The identity $F=F^3q(F)$ holds also on the nullspace, and the same $q$ works for the other portal. In a trace containing just one or two insertions, replace one $F$ using this identity and expand intermediate identities as $\sum_a P_a$. The resulting measured traces have between three and $N+3$ insertions. This recovers both low orders without resolving coincident sums of two masses or measuring a contact term. Traces containing only mass projections follow from orthogonality and their known ranks.

## Why these traces give exactly the stated ambiguity

Choose real diagonal mass projections. Any word in the projections and $F$ can be cyclically compressed using $P_aP_b=\delta_{ab}P_a$ and expanded with $I=\sum_aP_a$ between neighboring insertions. Its real trace is consequently available whenever its number of insertions is within the measured bound. For a second portal $F'$, evaluate each generator on the direct sum of the four tuples for $F,\overline F,F',\overline{F'}$. The complex space of block-diagonal word values has dimension at most $4N^2$. The span of words of length at most $k$ starts with the identity. If that span equals the span at $k+1$, multiplication by every generator leaves it invariant. It therefore stabilizes by $k=4N^2-1$. On this spanning set the trace of blocks one and two minus the trace of blocks three and four vanishes by the measured real traces. It then vanishes on the whole generated algebra.

The algebra is a finite-dimensional complex $*$-algebra because its generators are Hermitian. Its matrix summands have central identity projections. The trace of such a projection on either doubled representation is its simple matrix dimension times its multiplicity. Thus the doubled tuples for $F$ and $F'$ are unitarily equivalent. For each pair of inequivalent conjugate irreducibles $U,\bar U$, doubling records the sum of the two original multiplicities. For a self-conjugate irreducible the doubled multiplicity is twice the original, including the quaternionic case. These are exactly the choices obtained by conjugating individual summands and then transporting them by a unitary. The mass projections are part of the tuple, so the resulting unitary identification respects every prescribed mass space. Conversely, summands contribute additively to the trace, and conjugating any one summand preserves its real part at every order.

## A six-dimensional ambiguity

Take two identical copies of three distinct mass levels. In one copy use a zero-diagonal Hermitian triangle with $F_{12}=F_{23}=1$, $F_{31}=i$. Its mass projections and connected nonzero edges generate the full three-dimensional matrix algebra. Compare two copies of this triangle with one copy of it and one of its conjugate. Both portals have the same real trace words at every order. The complex trace of $P_1 F P_2 F P_3 F$ is $2i$ for the former and zero for the latter. It distinguishes these tuples even when an overall conjugation is allowed. The repeated mass spaces permit arbitrary unitary mixing of their two basis states, which also preserves this complex trace. [Exact matrix and interpolation checks](ch39-degenerate-portal-traces.py)

For a single mass block of known dimension $N$, consecutive normalized source orders $3$ through $N+2$ determine every signed Hermitian spectrum. The endpoint and the worst-case inverse exponent $1/(N+2)$ are sharp. [Counting-function proof and local multiplicity dependence](ch39-degenerate-portal-moment-order.md)

For two mass values with a smaller block of rank one or two, the sharp worst-case cutoffs are $\max(4,N+1)$ and $\max(6,2N-4)$, respectively. [Block reconstruction and sharp examples](ch39-low-rank-portal-words.md)
