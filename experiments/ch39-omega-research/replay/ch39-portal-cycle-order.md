# The sharp source order for a finite Hermitian portal

Consider $N\ge4$ complex Gaussian scalar fields in four Euclidean dimensions with the same internal quantum numbers, canonical kinetic terms, known pairwise distinct masses $\mu_i>0$, and an arbitrary Hermitian matrix $F$ multiplying a common real scalar source. At zero background, the connected one-loop responses at separated source points through order $2N-4$ determine $F$ up to diagonal rephasing and independent complex conjugation on each connected component of its off-diagonal support. This worst-case order is sharp even for connected support. Exact values at finitely many actual Euclidean configurations suffice. The bound counts insertions per response, with no claim of a small number of measurement configurations. [Four-layer finite sampling](ch39-four-source-portal-inverse.py)

## Reading a closed walk at separated points

At order $n\ge3$ the trace-log expansion and differentiation in $n$ labeled source locations give a sum over cyclic orders of these locations. The propagator on each edge of a cycle is $D_\mu(r)=\mu K_1(\mu r)/(4\pi^2r)$. Summing its mass labels gives terms for all species words $i_1,\ldots,i_n$, including repeated species. Fix an undirected Hamiltonian cycle of source positions with a mass on each edge. Its two orientations together supply the known common normalization times twice the real part of the product of the portal entries between consecutive mass labels. Cyclic repetitions from differentiating the trace have a known common multiplicity. The real part is independent of which orientation was chosen. Local contact terms have no support at these separated positions.

For noncoincident $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb R^4$ put $r_{ij}=|x_i-x_j|$. For each labeled kernel the exponential decay rate under $x_i\mapsto Lx_i$ is $a=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}r_{ij}$, with $c_{ij}$ equal to the assigned mass on cycle edges and zero elsewhere. Distinct kernels have distinct coefficient vectors. No nonzero real linear combination of the $r_{ij}$ can vanish identically on an open set. Its mixed Hessian in $x_i,x_j$ isolates $-c_{ij}(I-ee^\top)/r_{ij}$, where $e=(x_i-x_j)/r_{ij}$. Its transverse part is nonzero when $c_{ij}\ne0$. The distance functions are analytic on the connected ordered configuration space, so avoiding finitely many proper zero sets yields an actual configuration with all rates distinct.

Let $M$ be the number of distinct kernels at this order. At the scales $L,2L,\ldots,ML$ the radial asymptotic for column $j$ and row $k$ is $b_j(kL)^{-3n/2}\exp(-kLa_j)(1+O(L^{-1}))$ with $b_j>0$. Sort the distinct rates. The rearrangement inequality gives a unique permutation minimizing $\sum_k k a_{\pi(k)}$, pairing the largest rate with the smallest row. Its determinant term dominates for sufficiently large $L$, giving a nonsingular finite sampling matrix that depends only on the masses and positions. Solving the linear system recovers each real closed-walk product. Six explicit rational points and masses 1 and 2 give 3840 distinct rates in the [exact replay](ch39-portal-cycle-order.py). Their fifteen squared distances have distinct square-free parts. In the multiquadratic extension generated by the primes involved, the corresponding square roots belong to distinct character spaces and are linearly independent over the rationals.

## Recovering the relative phases on connected support

The third-order word $(i,i,i)$ determines the real diagonal $F_{ii}$ via its cube. The fourth-order word $(i,j,i,j)$ gives $|F_{ij}|^4$ and thus the full labeled support. In each component choose a spanning tree and use diagonal rephasing to make its edges positive. Each remaining edge, or chord, has a phase $\phi_e$ up to a fixed orientation. Its fundamental cycle, the chord together with the tree path joining its endpoints, has length at most $N$. Dividing its real product by the known product of positive edge moduli gives $\cos\phi_e$.

For two chords, if their tree paths share an edge their common part is a single path. Orient the cycles in opposite directions on this part. The complementary paths meet only at its endpoints, and their union is a simple cycle of length at most $N$ that reads $\cos(\phi_e\pm\phi_f)$, with the sign known from the orientations. If they share no tree edge, the two cycles meet in at most one vertex. Join them by a shortest path in the tree of length $d$, taking $d=0$ if they meet. Going around each cycle and traversing the connector in both directions gives a closed walk of length $h+k+2d$ and again reads $\cos(\phi_e\pm\phi_f)$. Here $h,k\ge3$ are the cycle lengths. Their union with the connector has $h+k+d-1\le N$ vertices, so $h+k+2d\le2N-4$.

Together with the individual cosines, the paired cosine gives $\sin\phi_e\sin\phi_f$ with known sign. Use one chord with nonzero sine as reference to determine all others up to simultaneous sign reversal. A chord with zero sine already has its phase fixed. Rephasing preserves the original component, and simultaneous sign reversal is complex conjugation. Each closed walk with a nonzero coefficient stays in one component. Independent conjugation on different components therefore preserves all orders. For instance two disconnected triangles with phases $\pi/2$ may be conjugated one at a time, yielding equal responses even though the results need not be related by one global conjugation.

## Sharp examples

For $N=4$, the chordless four-cycle has a loop phase invisible through third order. Set the diagonal to zero, keep all adjacent moduli one, and choose loop phases $0$ and $\pi/2$. For $N\ge5$, take two triangles joined by $d=N-5$ bridge edges. At $d=0$ they share one vertex. Give both triangles the phase $\pi/2$ and compare with the same graph after reversing just the second phase. The connected portals differ even after rephasing and overall conjugation. For a closed walk with signed winding numbers $r,s$ on the triangles the two phase factors have real parts $\cos((r+s)\pi/2)$ and $\cos((r-s)\pi/2)$. A difference requires both windings odd. This takes at least three traversals on each triangle and at least two on every bridge. Thus all lower orders agree. At length $6+2d=2N-4$, walking once around each triangle with the intervening round trip distinguishes the pair. The exact graph replay checks all connected labeled supports on four and five vertices and the extremal family up to sixteen vertices. The counting argument gives the bound for arbitrary $N$.
