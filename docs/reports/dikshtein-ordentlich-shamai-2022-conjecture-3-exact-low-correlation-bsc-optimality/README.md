# Double-sided bottleneck: exact low-correlation BSC checks

Target: Michael Dikshtein, Or Ordentlich and Shlomo Shamai (Shitz), “The Double-Sided Information Bottleneck Function”, Entropy 24(9):1321, DOI [10.3390/e24091321](https://doi.org/10.3390/e24091321), Conjecture 3. The intended crossover range is [0, 1/2]. Georg Pichler, [arXiv:2609.27991v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27991v1), Section 6, retains this question as open. These programs check finite cases and supply no universal proof.

Run `python3 check.py`. It uses only the standard library, with integers and rational arithmetic throughout. Logarithms of rational numbers are represented as rational linear combinations of prime logarithms; signs are certified by rational enclosures of the atanh series with an explicit remainder bound. It reproduces the DSBS law (11), the product-measure posterior identity of Lemma 3, and the BSC value of Proposition 5 in 125 exact cases. It checks all row-stochastic matrices of output sizes 1–3 with entries on the quarter grid, together with the stipulated BSCs, at two budget pairs and two crossovers. All 30,906 feasible pairs satisfy the proposed bound. `check.out` ends in `ALL_CHECKS_OK`.

The budgets are (1−h_b(1/4), 1−h_b(1/4)) and (1−h_b(1/8), 1−h_b(1/3)); crossovers are 49/100 and 99/200. The exact checker also checks the remainder coefficient bounds through index 128 and the proposed threshold identity at 256 positive rational values of M. None of these finite checks replaces the argument for arbitrary finite output alphabets or all budgets.

Run `python3 optimize.py` with NumPy 2.4.2 and SciPy 1.18.1 for a separate exploratory numerical check. It uses BA-type alternating constrained maximization, with SLSQP as the inner solver, six deterministic starts and output sizes 2–4. It records every aggregate solver-status count and the largest rate constraint residual. Across its 12 configurations, the largest objective excess above the BSC value is 2.65×10⁻¹⁴ bits and the largest budget excess is 8.05×10⁻¹⁰ bits. Some inner solves report unsuccessful statuses; these are retained in `optimize.out`. These floating-point readings provide no certificate of global optimality and are separate from the exact evidence in `check.py`.
