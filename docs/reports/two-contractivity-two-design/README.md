# Exact checks for two-contractivity and unitary 2-designs

These finite checks support the proposed unrestricted two-contractivity argument
for Wallman and Flammia, *Randomized Benchmarking with Confidence*,
arXiv:1404.6025v4, Definition 15 and the unnumbered conjecture preceding
Proposition 16 (DOI: 10.1088/1367-2630/16/10/103032). They are not a proof of
the universal conjecture.

Run with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14.0:

```sh
python3 docs/reports/two-contractivity-two-design/check.py
```

`check.out` is the program's complete successful output. Its final line is
`ALL_CHECKS_OK`. Arithmetic uses integers, SymPy rationals, exact radicals, and
the algebraic field Q(omega), with omega = (-1 + i sqrt(3))/2. No approximate
eigenvalues, tolerances, random choices, or floating-point evidence are used.

The program checks:

- The exact second-moment identity on all 16 qubit two-copy matrix units and
  all 81 qutrit Weyl tensor basis elements.
- The source's Lemma 6, Eq. (31), relating average gate fidelity to normalized
  Choi overlap, for nine qubit channels. Average fidelity is evaluated on the
  six Pauli eigenstates, a pure-state 2-design.
- The Choi-purity/ Liouville-norm identity, the averaged traceless Gram identity,
  and the full block characteristic-polynomial factorization, for 15 channels.
- A simple eigenvalue 1 and strict stability of all other eigenvalues for the
  12 nonunitary samples. Three unitary boundary samples instead have repeated
  eigenvalue 1, as required by the source's multiplicity convention.
- The auxiliary-map/ Kronecker identification on every 3-by-3 matrix unit in
  the nine qubit cases.

The complete parameter sets are qubit phase-flip probability
p in {0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1}, qubit damping amplitude
s in {0, 3/5, 4/5, 1}, and qutrit selective damping amplitude
s in {0, 3/5, 4/5}, with and without a following qutrit Fourier unitary.
Damping uses the rational pairs (s,t) = (0,1), (3/5,4/5), (4/5,3/5), (1,0)
with s^2+t^2=1. The selective qutrit Kraus matrices are
diag(1,s,1) and t|0><1|. Their unital projection is not positive: on |2><2|
its (0,0) output entry is -t^2/3. These samples therefore exercise the
higher-dimensional nonunital obstruction in the source.

The qubit design uses the 24 adjoint actions generated by Hadamard and the
phase gate. The qutrit design uses the 216 adjoint actions generated by the
Weyl shift X, clock Z, Fourier F, and P = diag(1,omega^2,omega^2). Generator
unitarity and the complete design identities are checked exactly. These
adjoint groups have finite unitary lifts: the determinants of the qubit
generators are fourth roots of unity, and those of the qutrit generators
are twelfth roots. A scalar in the kernel consequently has order dividing
8 or 36, respectively. Finiteness of the adjoint image and scalar kernel
implies finiteness of the generated unitary group. Equal-size central
cosets make the uniform adjoint average equal to the finite-group average.

For spectral certification, the program computes the full characteristic
polynomial over Q(omega), using its exact charge blocks, and verifies that
the resulting coefficients are rational. It removes one factor z-1 and
factors the residual over Q and applies the real Schur recursion to every factor, retaining every root and its multiplicity: for coefficients a_0,...,a_n in descending
order, require |a_n| < |a_0| and replace them by
a_0 a_j - a_n a_(n-j), j=0,...,n-1. Repeating to degree zero certifies that
every remaining root is strictly inside the unit disk. This uses the whole
spectrum; it does not substitute numerical diagonalization or assume the
matrix is diagonalizable.

The finite test domain is limited to these channels and designs in dimensions
2 and 3. The all-dimensional, all-channel, arbitrary-design argument remains
a mathematical outline for a later Lean probe.
