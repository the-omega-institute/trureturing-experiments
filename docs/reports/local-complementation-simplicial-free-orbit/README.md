# Local complementation and simplicial-free orbits

Run `python3 check.py` using Python 3.10 or later. Only the standard library is required. All graph operations, searches, isomorphism certificates and checks use integers and finite sets. The final output line is `ALL_CHECKS_OK`.

The source is O. Makuta, F. Baccari and R. Augusiak, *Genuine multipartite nonlocality detection in graph states*, arXiv:2610.13609v1. Section VI asks whether larger connected graphs can obstruct the clique-neighborhood construction of Theorem 3 throughout their local-complementation orbits. A vertex is simplicial when every two distinct neighbors are adjacent, including neighborhoods of size zero or one.

The fidelity checks reproduce the source's complete-four-vertex to star transformation and the exceptional six-vertex orbit of Figure 1 / Appendix E, class 19. That orbit has two isomorphism representatives and no simplicial vertex. The depicted triangular prism is checked to be isomorphic to an orbit representative of the wheel consisting of a five-cycle and its hub.

The new candidate consists of two disjoint copies of that wheel, with an additional edge between their hubs. Its twelve vertices are numbered 0 through 11; the hubs are 5 and 11. It is connected and has 21 edges. Exact breadth-first search modulo graph isomorphism returns 54 representatives and 648 transitions, one for every representative and every vertex. Every representative lacks a simplicial vertex.

`output.txt` contains the fidelity readings, a JSON object with the seed edges, all 54 adjacency-row arrays, all 648 transitions and all nonclique witnesses, then the success line. In an adjacency row, bit j means adjacency to vertex j. A transition `[i,v,j,p]` certifies that complementing representative i at v gives representative j under the vertex permutation p: adjacency at u,w in the complemented graph equals adjacency at p[u],p[w] in representative j. Nonclique witnesses give two nonadjacent neighbors for each vertex. Verification reconstructs every transition by independently toggling edge sets, checks every permutation entry and adjacency, and checks connectivity, absence of simplicial vertices and involutivity. Graph discovery uses backtracking isomorphism; its returned permutations are verified independently rather than trusted as yes/no answers.

For any relabeling p, local complementation commutes with p, and simpliciality is preserved by p. Induction on the length of a finite sequence therefore places every reachable labeled graph in one of the 54 listed isomorphism classes. Together with the listed nonclique witnesses this gives a computational obstruction for every finite sequence, not a sample of bounded-length sequences. This argument does not assert that there are only 54 labeled graphs or that the representatives are canonical labelings.

These are Python checks, with no Lean proof of the existence claim or its negation. Their principal mathematical content is a finite certificate for one fixed graph. The main repository's utility rule does not admit that positive certified instance merely because sequence induction consumes the certificate. A separate general structural argument is needed before a positive formalization can be proposed under `utility: none`.
