# Binary trinomial-power polycyclic codes: exact checks

Source: Sujata Bansal and Pramod Kumar Kewat, *On the Hamming Distance and LCD Properties of Binary Polycyclic Codes and Their Duals*, arXiv:2605.12097v1, Conjecture 1 and Example 6. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12097v1

Run with Python 3.10 or later, without external packages:

```sh
python3 check.py
```

The output is stored in `output.txt`. Integer bitmasks represent polynomials over F₂. Multiplication is carry-less, dot products use parity, and all ranks use exact Gaussian elimination over F₂. No floating-point calculation is used.

The fidelity check reproduces Example 6: the ideal generated by x³+x+1 modulo (x³+x+1)⁸ has parameters [24,21,2], its Euclidean dual has parameters [24,3,13], and its Gram rank is 21. Dual distance is checked by enumerating all eight dual words. Primal distance 2 is checked by excluding all singleton words by divisibility and exhibiting 1+x⁷.

For P=1+xᵃ+x²ᵃ, L=2ᵀ, and every j from 0 through L, the generator rows are xⁱPʲ, with 0≤i<2a(L−j). Both the generator rank and Gram rank are checked. The grids are a=1 with 1≤T≤7; a=2 or 3 with 1≤T≤4; a=9 with 1≤T≤2; and additionally 1≤a≤6 with 0≤T≤3. Their union contains 400 distinct codes, including endpoint codes. The first four grids contain 303 nontrivial codes and 34 endpoints.

The proposed gap-rigidity condition is exhaustively checked for all multiplier polynomials of degree less than a(L−j), for a=1 and T≤3, and for a=2 or 3 and T≤2, with 0<j<L: 950 tests. The characteristic-two power identity is checked on these grids. Two known non-LCD controls confirm that the checker can reject: (P,L,j)=(1+x+x²,3,1) and (1+x+x³,2,1).

These are exact finite checks. They do not establish the universal conjecture, certify originality, or constitute Lean kernel verification. The intended positive settlement uses a general polynomial argument.
