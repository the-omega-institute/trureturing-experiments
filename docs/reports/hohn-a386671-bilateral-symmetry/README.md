# Hohn A386671: an asymmetric primitive equal-length lattice walk

Run `python3 check.py`. The standard-library checker uses integers and exact rational arithmetic. Its saved output is `check.out`; success requires exit 0 and the final line `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These are computational checks, not a Lean settlement.

The mathematical sources are Charles L. Hohn's [OEIS A386671](https://oeis.org/A386671), revision 20 (18 August 2025), and its walk-definition clarification in [A385525](https://oeis.org/A385525), revision 32 (6 August 2025). The checker is original code and does not reuse the entry's PARI program. The 24-vector construction was supplied by research search seat `codex-a-r326`; this checker independently reconstructs it from three Gaussian-integer multiples of an eight-vector base.

The fidelity check reproduces the displayed A386671 eight-edge example: common length 40885, primitive edges, distinct unordered absolute-leg pairs, zero sum, bilateral symmetry after angle ordering, and total length 327080 as in A385525. It does not certify minimality or completeness of either sequence.

The counterexample has length 48612265 and 24 edges. The checker verifies nonzero coordinates, primitive edges, equal squared norms, pairwise noncongruence, exact closure, and increasing directed angle. All 528 support determinants at nonincident vertices are positive; their minimum is 90286825602984. Thus the partial sums form a strictly convex polygon. Both angle branches printed in the output describe the same polygon up to cyclic relabeling and translation.

Two independent exact tests exclude all reflection axes:

- A linear reflection is `z ↦ λ conjugate(z)`. Its image of the first edge must be one of 24 edges, determining λ exactly. All 24 candidates fail; the output gives a failing edge for each. A boundary reflection reverses orientation, so its negative linear part would permute the directed edges, and is again a reflection.
- Any affine boundary reflection fixes the rational vertex centroid and permutes the vertices. Only one vertex has the same centered squared norm as vertex 0. The unique resulting candidate also fails. This test uses `fractions.Fraction` throughout.

The modular mechanism gives a second mathematical route. Modulo 29 the first 16 vectors satisfy `y = -12x` and the last 8 satisfy `y = 12x`, with no zero vector. Since `12² = -1`, the two evaluations `x ± 12y` identify the split Gaussian algebra with two copies of the field. For equal-length vectors, a reflection makes `z·R(z)` constant. The presence of both classes forces both evaluations of that constant to vanish; consequently the reflection must swap the two classes. Their unequal counts prohibit this. The checker also verifies all 3136 products of nonzero isotropic vectors modulo 29.

An infinite-family construction needs no prime-existence assumption. Put `k = 48612265`, `t = 2k(m+3)` for any integer `m ≥ 0`, and multiply every edge by `u = (t²-1)+2ti`. Its norm is `C = t²+1`; the new length is `kC`. Since `t` is even, the coordinates of u are coprime; since k divides t, `gcd(C,k)=1`. If a prime divides both coordinates of a new edge, reduce either the multiplier or the original edge modulo that prime, according as the prime divides k or C. The other factor is invertible; this contradicts primitiveness. To exclude new congruences, rotations by Gaussian units commute with u and reduce to old congruences. A conjugating unit would require `(t²-1)A+2tB=0`, where A is a nonzero integral vector and both coordinates of B have absolute value at most `2k`. But `t>4k+1` gives `t²-1>4kt`, impossible in a nonzero coordinate of A. The same bound excludes axis-aligned edges. Similarity preserves strict convexity and absence of reflection; lengths strictly increase with m. Six exact samples are checked. These samples do not prove the infinite quantifier; the preceding symbolic argument supplies its proposed mechanism.

The planned Lean settlement is the single explicit counterexample, with utility `certified-instance` and basis `refutes`.
