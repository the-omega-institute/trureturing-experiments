# Skew planes with a finite noncyclic projection group

The target is Favacchio–Kettinger, *Collinearly complete sets and finite subgroups from configurations of skew n-planes in projective (2n+1)-space*, arXiv:2607.11259v1, Conjecture 3.19. The conjecture says that, over the complex numbers and for n > 1, finiteness of the collinearity-projection groupoid's isotropy group implies cyclicity.

Run `python3 check.py` with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14.0. The stored result is `output.txt`. The program uses exact integer, rational and Gaussian rational arithmetic throughout. It first reproduces Example 2.13: M = diag(2,3,5) gives 12 points in projective 5-space, with Hilbert values 1,6,9,12,12 and h-vector (1,5,3,3).

For the proposed refutation, put A = diag(1+i,1−i)/2, B = [[1,i],[i,1]]/2 and S = {0,I,A,B}. The program checks all pairwise determinants, closes the projective difference group, and independently closes its permutations of {∞,0,1,−1,i,−i}. Both closures have order 24, and A and B do not commute projectively. It solves the ambient incidence equations for all 60 primitive projections at block multiplicities 1,2,3; it also enumerates the complete connected groupoid from one base object, obtaining 24 morphisms to each of five targets.

For a positive integer k, Δ_k(M) is the block diagonal matrix with k copies of M. In W = (C²)^k take L∞ = P(0⊕W) and L_M = P{(u,Δ_k(M)u)}, for M in S. The six determinants before repetition are 1,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2; afterward they are their k-th powers. Thus the five planes are pairwise skew. Since Δ_k preserves differences, products and inverses, it preserves every primitive projection formula. In graph coordinates these formulas are I, M_i−M_k, (M_j−M_k)⁻¹, and (M_j−M_k)⁻¹(M_i−M_k), according to which index is ∞. Conversely each difference is an isotropy loop: transport from ∞ to L_i with coordinate class I through L_0 and L_I, then project back through L_j. The entire isotropy group is therefore the image of the projective difference group. Repetition is injective on projective classes: Δ_k(M) scalar implies M scalar. Consequently the isotropy group has order 24 for every k ≥ 2, giving a counterexample for every odd n = 2k−1 ≥ 3.

The finite program verifies the block identities and skewness for k = 2,...,6. The argument for every k in the preceding paragraph is a mathematical outline; finite enumeration does not verify an unbounded quantifier, and no Lean proof of the claim or its negation is provided here.

For k = 2, the vector v = (e₁,e₂) has a free 24-point projective orbit. The ordered columns v, Δ₂(A)v, Δ₂(B)v, Δ₂(AB)v have determinant −1/4. Each of the five transported orbits spans its plane; together the 120 points span projective 7-space. This is an additional nondegeneracy check, not an extra hypothesis of the conjecture.

Source definitions and matrix formulas: Section 2, Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.8 and Corollary 2.10 of arXiv:2607.11259v1. Noncyclic skew-line examples already occur in Favacchio, arXiv:2512.19811, including Example 4.6 of its revised text; the proposed higher-dimensional step is preservation of the full projection groupoid under block repetition.
